03 / THE METHOD

Make the claim checkable.
Keep its limits visible.

Our research prototype brings together a private customer ledger, a Merkle-sum tree, a zero-knowledge circuit and a public Ethereum contract.

01

Commit the ledger

TypeScript builds a fixed eight-leaf Poseidon2 tree. Each leaf commits to a customer identifier and balance; parent nodes bind the hashes and sums below them.

02

Prove the calculation

The Noir circuit constrains balances to unsigned 64-bit values and calculates the root and total. The contract verifies the proof before accepting an epoch.

03

Compare the reserves

The registry compares ETH at its configured reserve addresses with the proven total. Address control, asset eligibility and undisclosed obligations remain separate questions.

04

Check your inclusion

Your proof reconstructs the path to the published root. This portal also checks your expected customer ID, expected balance and the committed liability total.

What this portal does not certify

Complete liability disclosure, legal ownership of reserves, locked funds, future withdrawals, or the integrity of an arbitrary contract supplied by a user. The selected RPC and contract must be trusted independently. Inclusion proofs can reveal sibling subtree totals.
